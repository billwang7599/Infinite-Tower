# ART.md

How the game's art should look, and how to make it. All the game's art is code: the game loads no image files. The
portrait is traced from Stable Diffusion pictures (`client/art/sd/`, dev only) into tone grids in the source;
everything else is drawn in code. Hand-drawn art by an artist is a plan for later ([plan/art.md](plan/art.md)); the
art lab's pixel editor can draw it, but nothing it makes is in the game yet.

## Direction

- **Pixel art, JRPG with Arknights' taste**: clean anime faces, cel shading, muted clothes with one accent. Pretty,
  not realistic: big heads and eyes, tiny noses and mouths. Bodies are athletic, never heavy.
- **An old, worn world.** Warm skin on cool, near-neutral, greyed everything else (soot, ash, slate, oxblood, moss,
  old leather, tarnished brass); one bright accent per character on small trims; the eyes the brightest thing.
- **Hard pixels.** Scaled by whole numbers, no smoothing, no anti-aliasing inside a sprite, no gradients: a few flat
  tones with hard-edged shadows. Light comes from the top left.
- **Outlines are the darkest tone of their own colour**, softer inner lines where parts of one material meet.
- **Portraits are three-quarter views**, never front-facing; that's what makes a small face look alive.
- **Feel over looks in the arena** (AGENTS.md): the 3D people's work goes into the skeleton, motion and physics.
- **First person first**: the fight is tuned to read from the eye; third person need only not look broken.

## Characters

Every character is one look drawn several ways:

- **Portrait**: a head-and-shoulders bust for dialogue, creation, the character panel and the HUD's card.
- **Chibi**: the overworld sprite, about 1.7 heads tall, facing front, back or side (mirrored for the other side).
- **Battle sprite**: the arena's 2D figure from behind or the front, in a few poses.
- **3D person**: in the arena, players and people are simple 3D models built from the look's colours and the battle
  sprite's proportions, posed in code; enemies are lofted 3D models.

**Colours are ramps.** Every material is a ramp of six tones (`gfx/look/palette.ts`); pictures record each pixel's
material and tone and are coloured last, so a new hair colour or dye is a new ramp and nothing else.

**How a character is built.** Everything visible is an `Art` (`gfx/look/art.ts`): per view, a draw function per layer
it shows on, drawn bottom to top, then outlined and coloured. One head is shared by everyone; face shapes change only
the jaw; body types are frames with the same named shapes and landmarks. Gear is drawn against the frame, never at
fixed pixels, so one drawing fits every body. The portrait's tone grids are generated by a script from the Stable
Diffusion pictures: change a picture and rerun it, never edit the grids by hand.

**What to draw** multiplies: a wrap-around piece needs a drawing for every body type, view, facing and battle pose;
a pinned piece (a helmet, a weapon) one per view; face shapes, hair, eyes and mouths only their own views. Gender
changes no art, and colour options cost no drawings. Keep body types, views and poses few for that reason.

**The 3D people** share one rig (`SKELETON`, joints named as in Blender) and one body lofted to the sprite's
proportions; the creator's face is painted on the head; hair and clothes are swappable parts; worn gear shows on
them. Poses are data (`CLIPS`), written for the right hand and mirrored. Motion matters most: keys reached and then
crept, swings along one arc, feet planted, every change of pose eased so nothing pops. In first person only the arms
and what they hold show, always in view, and a blade never swings back through your head.

**Adding a look option, gear, a body type or a pose**: add it to its table (`LOOK_OPTIONS`, the Art lists,
`BODY_TYPES`, `POSES`) with a piece for every view; it won't compile until the art exists. Check it in the art lab's
Characters tab, from the front too.

## Other art

- **Title screen**: a low-poly glowing white and gold tower in a dark-fantasy night, drawn on a 2D canvas.
- **HUD and menus**: "Inscribed" (the user's pick): inscriptions cut into stone, sharp over the pixel art. Carved bars
  with pointed ends, cut corners, Gill Sans capitals, bone text and a cinnabar accent, the foe's bar red. The tokens
  are in `client/src/ui/styles.css`.
- **The overworld**: HD-2D, pixel sprites and props on 3D ground; Highmere's walls and houses are 3D volumes whose
  depth comes from geometry, never painted shading. It lives by small motion: plants lean in the wind, people standing
  breathe a pixel, and anything that moves or turns is eased, never snapped.
- **The arena**: outlined pixel art whose pixels shrink with depth but never to dust, so far things still read as
  pixel art. What reads as HUD (the aim ring, crosshair, lock-on, chant words) is drawn sharp over it. Arenas keep the
  fight's ring clear and put their depth outside it, with flat far sprites that face the camera.
- **Items**: one bag picture each, a fixed pixel size per bag cell, in the same kit and rules as the characters.

## Don'ts

- No pure black, no pure-white outlines, no colours outside a ramp, no half-pixels.
- No gear drawn per character, or that fits only one hair style or body type.
- No bright, clean, saturated clothes or shiny new metal.

## The art lab

`npm run art`, then http://localhost:5175. A dev tool, never shipped: nothing it makes goes into the game yet, and
nothing is kept in the browser, so save drawings as files.

- **Characters tab**: every look option in every view, pose and facing, a mock dialogue and random looks; the place to
  check new art.
- **Pixel editor tab**: draw sprites, tiles or animations on the game's canvas sizes (or any size), with Aseprite-like
  tools and fixed shortcuts (pencil, eraser, fill, eyedropper, line, rectangle, select; undo and redo), the game's
  colour ramps, and layers.
- **Tiles mode** previews a tile repeated so seams show; **Animation mode** has frames, onion skin and playback. Both
  export a sheet PNG with a JSON of its cells.
- **Templates and parts** start a drawing from the art the game draws today, in layers per part, or one part alone on
  a base model underlay, so new art fits the game's.
- **Your own pictures**: open PNGs pixel for pixel, or import a photo snapped to the game's ramps to trace over.
- **Saving** writes `name.json` and `name.png` into `art-lab/files/` (committed to git; `ART_LAB_FILES_DIR` points
  elsewhere). There's no autosave.
